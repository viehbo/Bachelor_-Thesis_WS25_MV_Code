\chapter{Use Case}
\label{ch:use_case}

The use case demonstrates how the tool can support exploratory preselection of meteorological variables for glacier related machine learning tasks. The objective is not to prove causality, but to assess whether wind magnitude and/or direction exhibits spatial or temporal patterns that justify further modeling.

\section{Hypothesis}

The expected pattern is, that wind speed and direction changed over the years, to a stronger wind from south. This significantly influences the glacier melt. \\

This hypothesis is based on the assumption that due to general higher temperatures, the atmospheric pressure over the Mediterranean Sea increases on average, compared to the Northern Alps region. And as a result, more south Foehn days are seen over the main ridge, which causes temporarily high temperatures in affected areas.

The first remarkable pattern is that areas of high wind can only be found in flat regions. The western area of Munich and the south-western area near Vienna are most prominent.

This is unexpected. High wind speeds are typically associate with high mountains. But along the main ridge, the wind speed is lowest in average.

One possible explanation is that averaging over all time steps causes winds from different directions to partially cancel out, reducing the apparent magnitude in regions with variable wind directions.
To investigate this further, two specific grid points are selected for comparison: one near Vienna and one in Eastern Tirol.

\paragraph{Findings}

The wind speed shows no clear trend over the years in this data, and no
obvious influence on the glacier mass balance is visible. The glacier
trend line is fitted and positioned using a multiplier and an offset to allow visual comparison on the same axis.

This suggests that the 10\,m wind components do not carry sufficient information to serve as predictors for glacier mass balance.
However, the broader ERA5 family offers numerous additional wind-related variables such as wind at pressure levels, wind gusts, or directional statistics that may better capture the relevant atmospheric dynamics.
Identifying which variables lack predictive value early in the process avoids committing computational resources to unsuitable
features and helps narrow the search toward more promising candidates.
